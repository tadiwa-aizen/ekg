\begin{abstract}
Event knowledge graphs (EKGs) are critical for representing
complex event-related data and capturing temporal and causal
relationships among events and entities. Despite the growing body of research and applications of EKGs, a significant gap remains in their evaluation.  Current measures often address only a subset of necessary features, varying significantly between studies and projects. This which results in incomplete assessments that fail to encapsulate all critical aspects of EKGs. This research aims to address this gap by developing a comprehensive evaluation framework specifically tailored for event
knowledge graphs.This framework integrates existing evaluation methods while introducing new metrics to address gaps in traditional assessments, ensuring a comprehensive and simultaneous evaluation of the temporal, causal,  contextual, and several other critical nuances inherent to EKGs. By systematically analyzing existing evaluation methods, identifying their limitations, and proposing a
more suitable approach, this project seeks to enhance the accuracy
and relevance of EKG evaluations. The proposed framework will
facilitate more robust assessments, guiding future research and applications in the domain of event-centric knowledge representation.

We implement the framework as an open-source tool, \texttt{ekg-eval-cli}, and validate it against three synthetic event graphs with known ground truth. Experimental results demonstrate that the framework successfully captures multidimensional EKG quality across nine evaluation dimensions with 32 metrics, detecting a clear quality gradient across datasets: temporal coverage ranges from 100\% to 57.50\%, external mapping from 100\% to 8.33\%, and label uniqueness from 10.31\% to 2.04\%. Critically, the framework distinguishes between datasets exhibiting quality trade-offs across dimensions and datasets with uniform degradation---a distinction invisible to narrow evaluation approaches.
\end{abstract}
