Project Proposal: Developing A Standard Evaluation Framework for
Event-Centric Knowledge Graphs
Tadiwa Magwenzi
University Of Cape Town
MGWTAD001@myuct.ac.za
ABSTRACT
Event knowledge graphs (EKGs) are critical for representing
complex event-related data and capturing temporal and causal
relationships among events and entities. High-quality evaluations
of EKGs are crucial for ensuring their effectiveness and reliability
in real-world applications. EKG’s are designed to capture and
represent the complex relationships and sequences of events,
which are vital for tasks like event prediction, and causal analysis.
Due to this, a robust evaluation framework is needed assess how
accurately an EKG models these events, their temporal dynamics,
and interdependencies. Without rigorous evaluation, the EKG
may provide misleading insights, potentially leading to faulty
decision-making in critical domains such as finance, healthcare,
and cybersecurity. Despite the growing body of research and
applications of EKGs, a significant gap remains in their
evaluation. Current measures often address only a subset of
necessary features, varying significantly between studies and
projects. This which results in incomplete assessments that fail to
encapsulate all critical aspects of EKGs.
My research aims to address this gap by developing a
comprehensive evaluation framework specifically tailored for
event knowledge graphs. This framework integrates existing
evaluation methods while introducing new metrics to address
gaps in traditional assessments, ensuring a comprehensive and
simultaneous evaluation of the temporal, causal, contextual, and
several other critical nuances inherent to EKGs. By systematically
analyzing existing evaluation methods, identifying their
limitations, and proposing a more suitable approach, this project
seeks to enhance the accuracy and relevance of EKG evaluations.
The proposed framework will facilitate more robust assessments,
guiding future research and applications in the domain of event-
centric knowledge representation

1. RESEARCH DESCRIPTION
Event knowledge graphs (EKGs) are advanced data structures
designed to represent and organize information around events
ratherthan static entities.[1] Unlike traditional knowledge graphs,
which focus primarily on entities and their relationships, EKGs
emphasize the temporal, causal, and contextual connections
between events and the entities involved. This makes EKGs
particularly effective in domains where understanding the
sequence, cause, and impact of events is crucial, such as in news
analytics, historical data analysis, and event-driven systems. For
instance, ECKGs constructed from news articles can capture
dynamic changes over time, helping users reconstruct complex
storylines and explore hidden patterns that are often overlooked in
traditional knowledge graphs [2].
Traditional Knowledge Graphs are designed to represent factual
information about entities (such as people, places, organizations,
etc.) and their relationships. The primary goal is to provide a
comprehensive and structured representation of knowledge, often
used for tasks like semantic search, question answering, and
information retrieval. While EKG’s focus specifically on
representing events and the relationships between them. The aim
is to capture the temporal, spatial, and causal relationships
between events, providing a dynamic understanding of sequences
and interactions that occur over time. [1] The two types of
knowledge graphs differ in a myriad of ways, one of the most
important is the way they represent relationships. Traditional KGs
emphasize static relationships between entities, such as "Person A
works at Company B" or "City X is in Country Y." The
relationships are often more static and do not inherently
incorporate time as a dimension. In contrast, EKG’s emphasize
dynamic relationships, including temporal (when events happen),
causal (what causes what), and spatial (where events occur)
relationships. For example, "Event A leads to Event B," "Event X
occurs after Event Y," or "Event Z happens at Location L." [4].
EKGs solve several critical issues by capturing the dynamic
nature of information. They provide a more nuanced
understanding of how events unfold over time and how they
influence each other, offering insights into causality and sequence
that are often missed by entity- centric models. For example, in
news analytics [2] EKGs can help trace the development of a
story, identify pivotal moments, and understand the relationships
between different news events and entities involved. This enables
more accurate trend analysis, event prediction, and contextual
understanding.
Traditional KGs emphasize static factual relationships (e.g.,
"Person A works at Company B"). They do not capture complex
event relationships such as causality, temporality, or sequence of
events (e.g., "Event X causes Event Y, which happens after Event
Z"). This makes it difficult to use them for applications like
decision-making or scenario planning that rely on understanding
the progression and dependencies of events. To address this,
EKGs go beyond simple entity-entity relationships by
incorporating event-event relationships (such as causal, temporal,
and conditional relationships) and event- entity relationships. This
makes them more suitable for applications requiring deep
reasoning about events, such as predicting outcomes,
understanding causality, and generating timelines. [1]
Traditional KGs are also not well-suited to incorporate the
context surrounding events, such as the circumstances under
which an event occurred or its impact. This makes it challenging
to perform more nuanced reasoning or to extract insights that
depend on understanding the full context of an event sequence.
EKG’s, however, provide a framework to represent the context
and attributes associated with each event, such as participants,
locations, and time frames. This enhanced contextual
understanding allows for more accurate and nuanced reasoning,
improving the quality of insights that can be extracted. [4]
The current evaluation methods for Event Knowledge Graphs
tend to focus on assessing specific features, such as accuracy of
event representation, temporal relations, or completeness of
spatio-temporal queries, in isolation rather than providing a
comprehensive evaluation of all relevant aspects. Metrics like
Precision and Recall are often used to evaluate the accuracy of
event relationships by comparing system-generated graphs with
manually annotated ones, while other methods, like the use of
Cohen’s Kappa Coefficient or Specific Triple Accuracy, assess
the consistency and semantic richness of event references in
news data [2]. Some approaches, like the ones used to evaluate
AugGKG[5], evaluate completeness and efficiency in handling
complex queries within geographic contexts. However, these
methods usually address one or two dimensions—such as
event sequencing, causal relationship extraction, or multilingual
support—without encompassing the full set of features that define
an ECKG. Thus, while existing evaluation techniques provide
valuable insights, there is a need for more holistic approaches that
consider the diverse and interconnected attributes of ECKGs to
ensure a balanced and thorough assessment of their capabilities.
Given these limitations, there is a clear need for a novel
evaluation framework tailored specifically for ECKGs that can
comprehensively assess their multifaceted nature. A robust
evaluation framework must go beyond traditional metrics to
incorporate new measures that capture the graph’s ability to
handle dynamic event evolution, support inferential reasoning,
maintain consistency across diverse data sources, and represent
events in a way that is both semantically rich and contextually
accurate. Such a framework would enable a more nuanced
understanding of an ECKG’s strengths and weaknesses, guiding
improvements in their construction and application. It would also
provide a standardized way to compare different models, fostering
advancements in ECKG research and development by
highlighting which features are being successfully addressed and
which require further innovation.
Ultimately, a comprehensive evaluation framework would ensure
that ECKGs are fully capable of supporting the complex decision-
making and analytical needs of real-world applications.
Thus, the primary objective of this research is to develop a
comprehensive evaluation framework for event knowledge
